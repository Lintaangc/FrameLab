About digital image processing

A short orientation for reading the results.

An image is a matrix

A digital image is a grid of pixels. In 8-bit images each channel stores an intensity from 0 (black) to 255 (full brightness). Processing means computing new numbers from the old ones.

Three families of operations

Point operations change each pixel alone (brightness, negative, gamma, thresholding). Neighbourhood operations look at nearby pixels (blur, edges). Geometric operations move pixels (mirroring, rotation).

The histogram tells the story

A histogram counts how many pixels have each intensity. A narrow histogram means low contrast, one piled to the right means a bright image. Watch how it moves after each method.
